House style, without the book

What we decided

Stop waiting for the Sapiens file. It is not on this Mac and may never arrive. The task was blocking the writing, and CLAUDE.md says never block. Three parallel Sonnet researchers rebuilt the method from Goodreads verbatim quote banks, an erenow.org full-text mirror, LitCharts guides, the NPR excerpt and the critical literature. Raw output in data/harari-structure.md, data/harari-sentences.md, data/harari-reframes.md. That is enough to write a usable style guide.

"Zooming out" is four teachable techniques, not a temperament. This was the real answer to Matt's question. The pendulum (n=1 straight to n=all, never the middle distance); the reframe (reverse the load-bearing word of an unexamined belief); thesis-first chapter shape (title is a verdict, not a topic); and sentence compression (long reasoning, short verdict). The key mechanical insight: Harari lands a zoom-out by naming the pattern, not by adding cases. That is also exactly why critics call him a just-so storyteller — the power and the danger are the same move.

We hedge harder than he does. Eight specific charges against Sapiens are all evidence handling; none touch the rhetorical technique. So the style guide takes the method and bans the sin. The commercial argument, not just the ethical one: our reader already fears they cannot trust their own grasp of history. One caught error confirms that fear. Harari survives Hallpike on platform size. We would not.

35,000 words, 8 chapters of ~4,000. Sapiens is ~6,000 a chapter over 20, and "too dense, no chance to absorb" is the reader complaint in our own demand research.

  • A generic "popular history writing" guide. It would have been platitudes, and Matt asked what zooming out actually means.
