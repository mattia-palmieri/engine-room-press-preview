The plainness run. AI judges cannot measure "easy to understand", so plainness became a mechanical filter; the Surplus blurb shipped as the staff-only door.

Blurb plainness — 2026-09-03

What Matt said

He rejected the run-1 winner (the Turin grain-strike blurb): "i think there is a missing criteria. it must be easy to understand. i dont understand the new proposed book summary. its too abstract and complex."

What we decided

  • PLAIN is a mechanical filter, not a judged score. Added as criterion 4, then re-run blind — and the judges scored the very blurb Matt had just rejected 2.67/3 on plainness and ranked it first. A model parses a dense sentence at no cost, so it cannot feel the cost. Counting works; asking does not. Limits: 85 words, no sentence over 22 words, one lookup-able proper noun, three links of cause. Applied before any judge sees a draft.
  • The force is the subject; the picture is only proof. Run 2's plainest, best-liked draft opened on the invention of writing, and all three judges said "a book about why writing was invented" — a different book on our list. Check the judges' "a book about ___" answers against the catalogue before shipping.
  • Shipped the staff-only door blurb for Surplus. Unanimous 3.00 PLAIN, 2/3 on the $9 gate. The one No was the Sapiens-reader on novelty, not comprehension.

What we tried and abandoned

  • Judging plainness. Three personas, including a deliberately unforgiving non-native phone reader. All three still cleared the rejected blurb. Do not re-try a judged plainness score.
  • B3 and E2/E3 were cut by the word/sentence counter before judging. Enforce the filter in code, not in the brief — writers overshoot by one or two words.

Open questions

  • Leverage and "don't name it" fight each other. Run 1 says gesture at the present-day thing; the leverage criterion scores 3 only when a judge names one unprompted. Nothing hit 3/3 in run 3 because of it. Resolve before rewriting the other fifteen.
  • The other fifteen blurbs have not been through the counter. Most will break the 85-word or 22-word limits. That is a queued job, not done.
  • Matt's yes/no on this blurb still closes blurb-eval-surplus.
