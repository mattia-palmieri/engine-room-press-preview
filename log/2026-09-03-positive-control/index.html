Run 6 — the eval fails its own control

What we decided

The buy/no-buy eval is no longer a gate. It stays as a house-style checker. No blurb ships on its say-so again. hooks.md rewritten to say so; the four writing rules are kept but relabelled house style, not predictions.

Why. Run 6 did the thing five earlier runs never did: it compared our blurbs to something outside our own drafts. Nine blurbs, blind, no titles — OSTIA (live), DOOR and TURIN (both rejected by Matt in his own words), against six real publisher blurbs from Salt, The Box, Prisoners of Geography, Material World, Stuff Matters and An Edible History of Humanity, cut by deletion only to comparable length with names and awards stripped.

Result: ours took 8 of 9 $9-Yes votes, the six real blurbs took 3 of 18 — and TURIN, the blurb Matt called "too abstract and complex", scored a perfect 3.00 for plainness from all three judges and a unanimous Yes. DOOR, which he said he did not understand at all, scored 3.00 for plainness and 3.00 for hype.

The judges are not generous — they refused five of six real blurbs. They are circular. Asked what their Yes blurbs shared, all three described hooks.md almost clause for clause: open on a physical scene not a claim, close on one quotable line, no adjectives doing the persuading. The rubric was derived from our drafts, so our drafts were the answer key.

The active item changed shape. It is no longer "write a better Surplus blurb". It is get one blurb Matt would buy, from any book, anywhere. We hold three Matt verdicts and all three are No; a predictor cannot be calibrated on negatives alone, because a judge that says No to everything scores 100% on an all-No set.

What we tried and abandoned

Run 7, another rewrite. Five runs, three rejections, every run scored by the instrument under test. Stopped.

A "Matt simulator" judge built from his rejection quotes. Considered and dropped for the same reason: unvalidatable against an all-No set.

Concluding our copy beats real bestsellers. It does not follow and it is a trap. A real jacket blurb never works alone — famous name, bestseller sticker, 4,000 reviews, all of which we stripped. Run 6 shows our rubric ranks them last. That condemns the rubric, not the books.

Open questions

  • Still Matt's, and now the whole item: name one blurb, from any book, that made him want to read it.
  • surplus-premise is untouched and run 6 does not weaken it — Judge 3 still called DOOR "one clever idea stretched thin".
  • The only outside finding worth keeping: The Box was the one real blurb that beat the field, and it opens on a dated event with numbers in it — no claim, no importance, no adjective.
  • The other fifteen blurbs have still never been through the plainness counter. That debt is unchanged, but it now matters less: the counter is a style tool, not a gate.
