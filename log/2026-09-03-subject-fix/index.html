Run 5 — the subject fix, and the ending trade turns out to be worth nothing

Two questions in one run, both left open by run 4.

What we decided

The subject rule, and it works. A reader who has read only the first two sentences must already be able to say what the book is about. Four writers' worth of drafts written under that rule: all eight versions scored 3.00 on subject from all three judges. The blurb it replaced — which opened on a locked staff-only door and reached the grain in sentence four — has scored 2 from every judge in every run since run 2. This is now a hard rule in hooks.md and it applies to all sixteen blurbs.

Shipped: the Ostia warehouse blurb (A3P). It beats the blurb it replaces on three criteria and ties the other two — subject 2.33 → 3.00, leverage 2.00 → 2.33, plain 2.67 → 3.00, hype 3.00 and the $9 gate 2/3 both unchanged. Live in data/covers/covers.json, docs/catalogue.md and on /covers/.

The ending trade is a tie, and run 4's headline rule is dead. Run 4 said stay in the past to the last word. Run 5 tested it as a matched pair: four blurbs, each written twice, word-for-word identical except the final sentence (one ancient close, one reaching into the present). The $9 gate came out five Yes each way. The forward-reaching close won leverage in 4 pairs of 4; the ancient close won hype. Nobody's buying decision moved. hooks.md rule 1 is rewritten to say so.

What we tried and abandoned

Run 4's finding, and why it was wrong. It compared leverage scores across different blurbs, so the ending was confounded with everything else in them. The matched pair is the instrument; anything measured across different drafts is a correlation, not a cause. Worth remembering the next time this eval produces a confident rule.

Judging all eighteen versions. Nine candidates is already at the edge of what a one-read judge can do carefully. Four pairs plus the control were carried; the other five pairs were dropped on the subject-anchor test before judging, which is editorial and is written down as such in assemble_r5.py.

Open questions

The real ceiling is the premise, not the prose. Judge 3, the Sapiens reader, said No to all nine candidates — control included — with the same sentence nine times: "it's still the surplus-equals-power idea I already own." He is not a No-machine; he was a Yes in run 1. So the 3/3 pass bar is unreachable for this book however well the blurb is written. That is the eval's own loop step 3 firing: when every draft fails the same way, the fault is the book's premise. New todo in the queue: surplus-premise. It probably affects several of the other fifteen.

Still Matt's, unchanged: yes/no on the shipped blurb, and clicking the FormSubmit activation email — until he does, every waitlist signup is dropped silently.
