Chapter one of The Surplus

What we decided

The chapter argues appropriability, not abundance. The schoolbook arrow — farming → plenty → leisure → kings — is reversed. What made states was not more food, it was food that could not be hidden: grain ripens in the open, all at once, and then sits countable in one heap. The reframe sentence is "a surplus is not food you have, it is food somebody else can find."

The book's coinage is stored hunger — grain in a store is not food, it is other people's future hunger bought in advance. One coinage per book, defined where it is coined, reused unchanged from here on. Nothing else gets coined in this book.

The honesty clause is Çatalhöyük, and it is in the body, not the back matter. Amy Bogaard's household-level storage evidence is the strongest thing against this book's whole argument — a thousand years of stored grain and no king. It is stated plainly, because burying it is exactly what house-style.md §5 forbids. Miriam Stark's Angkor objection and Opie & Atkinson's fourteen non-grain states are in for the same reason.

What we tried and abandoned

  • The Kushim tablet. It is Harari's worked example. Uruk is reached instead through Englund's accounting percentages (under 1% of the earliest tablets are anything but accounts) and the bevelled-rim ration bowl.
  • An opening scene. House style bans it. The chapter opens on a flat claim at maximum scale; Claudius arrives in section two as the payoff.
  • "The stores held years of grain." See below — it is false.
  • The /taste/ row on the hub. Dropped test, removed from the site so it stops inviting three minutes of Matt's time on something we abandoned.

The error we found in our own copy

catalogue.md said Ostia's grain store held "enough to feed the whole city for years." False. The best-documented warehouse ground floor is ~6,000–7,000 tonnes against a city eating 150,000–400,000 tonnes a year, and Tacitus has Rome down to fifteen days of food in AD 51. The horrea buffered a shipping season, not a famine. catalogue.md is rewritten with the true version, which is a much better hook: fifteen days is more frightening than "years".

This is the second time a self-written claim went unchecked until someone looked. It is the same failure mode as the eval and the rejected surfaces.
