Design system — Engine Room Press

What we decided

  • Strategy before execution. Wrote data/design/brief.md first: what the reader feels (quietly stupid, afraid of a 600-page book), what we sell (the click of seeing machinery), and the competitive default to differ from.
  • The anti-list is the real decision. Banned outright: parchment, sepia, gold serif capitals, marble, engraved maps, dark academia — the universal history-brand look, which says "old, important, heavy", the three words our reader bounced off. Also banned: gradient/rounded-card SaaS look (says app, not book), emoji, icon triples, faces, trivia framing.
  • The signature device must come from the product's story — hidden machinery made visible — not be an ornament. Each direction proposes one.

Three directions, identical copy

Same headline, promises, capture block and Instagram card in all three, so the judgement is about design and not writing. Each direction ships a landing hero AND a true 1080x1350 card, because the loud surface and the quiet one must read as one brand.

  • A — Technical drawing: engineering cutaway, thin rules, numbered callouts on leader lines. Device: the numbered callout.
  • B — The ledger: ruled account book, everything an entry on a line, tabular figures. Device: the ruled row.
  • C — The plate: mid-century Pelican paperback, one loud flat colour, oversized printed numeral. Device: the plate number.

Files: data/design/direction-{a,b,c}.html plus a short .md rationale each.

What we tried and abandoned

Nothing abandoned yet. Noted for the next session: do NOT judge from the stylescapes alone — the skill's own warning is that stylescapes always look good. The tie-break is the Instagram card at feed size and the page at phone width.

The verdict — Direction A wins

All three were built and screenshotted, then judged on the landing page and the 1080x1350 card at once. Instagram is our only channel, so the card was the tie-break.

  • A, Technical drawing — WINS. Its device (numbered callouts on leader lines) is the only one derived from the business's own story rather than from a genre, and it holds at both volumes.
  • B, The Ledger — rejected. Best landing page of the three, genuinely handsome editorial serif. Its card goes quiet where we need volume and reads as a finance newsletter. A weak card is disqualifying however good the page is.
  • C, The Plate — rejected as a whole, absorbed in part. Loudest card, best stopping power, but the oversized numeral sat on the headline and ate it. Kept: cards must be a full-bleed flat colour field with type far larger than feels comfortable, and its blue #173B4E survives as a second field colour so a 10-card grid has rhythm.

The system is A's construction at C's volume on the card. A hybrid of validated pieces — the skill's own words — not a compromise.
