What we decided (the four canonical docs)

All four are new, all four have a matching DECISIONS.md entry with the rejected alternatives.

  • positioning.md — the reader is the adult who bounced off history at school and now can't follow the news, not "curious adults". The promise: one force per book, no dates to memorise, finished in an evening. We signal the Harari promise using the readers' own Reddit words, never his name.
  • distribution.md — Instagram only, 5 static image cards a week, one bio link to an email capture page. TikTok and Shorts parked, not rejected. Funnel: ~450 profile visits per paying stranger. Kill tripwire written.
  • offer.md — ~20k-word ebook, $4.99 (KDP 70% band, nets ~$3.44) plus $12.99 paperback. Free rung is the real first three chapters, not a lead magnet. Amazon first because Amazon supplies the stranger; direct sale second.
  • plan.md — 30 days, week by week, with Matt's own six jobs listed.

The two judgement calls worth remembering

  1. The faceless conflict is resolved, not compromised. Every scaled history newsletter is a named person — true, and it looked fatal. But that is a channel fact, not a business fact: a newsletter is a relationship with a person, while faceless short video wins at multi-million scale in this exact niche. So the imprint stays faceless and builds on Instagram; the email list is the destination, not the brand.
  2. The curriculum gap is real but is not the opening move. "History as a curriculum you finish" genuinely does not exist for adults. It stays out of the ladder: nothing exists to price it against (not found, not a low number — nothing at all), it is the heaviest possible build, and it breaks the brief's own distribution-before-production rule. It arrives at 3-4 books.

What we tried and abandoned

  • Letting a research agent report findings into my context. Every agent this run was ordered to write a file and return at most 3-5 lines. That held.
  • A subagent that spawns children and then exits. Happened twice — the parent returned "my children are running, I'll wait" and died. Both times the fix was to SendMessage the parent once the children's files landed and tell it to compile now. Watch for this: a parent agent cannot wait on its own children.
  • The browser agent is the fragile one. Round 1's Amazon agent produced no file at all. Round 2's relaunch took 25 minutes and 135 tool calls but delivered everything. Always check for the browser agent's file first.
