Covers: colour and texture

The miss that started it

Matt said this on 2026-09-03: "d-bookshop is good. on textures, we're not there yet, theyre too subtle. lets play more with colors, texture, we want to showcase our brand identity, not something bland and boilerplate." The next round answered only the blurb half of his message and never opened the texture half. It was not in the queue either, so it nearly died. Lesson: when Matt's message carries two asks, queue the one you are not doing this round before you reply.

What we decided

Four parts, four field colours, four ISO 128 material hatches. Every part of the catalogue is now a different material cut open:

Part Field Material Opacity
I Tools #14171A iron black steel, even 45° section hatch 24%
II Communication #173B4E steel blue glass, alternating long/short 45° 14%
III Stories #17342A oxidised copper weave, cross-hatch 13%
IV Power #251830 aubergine concrete, stipple + sparse strokes 14%

Why this and not just a louder hatch: it answers both halves of the note at once, and the texture now means something the imprint's own name already claims. The ink field needed 24% against the others' 13–14% because a white hairline on near-black has the lowest apparent contrast — the numbers were tuned on rendered thumbnails, not matched on paper.

What we tried and abandoned

  • Sixteen colours, four families of four (options/palette/dir-b.png). The most colourful and the one that fails on brand: warming Part I turns The Barrel and The Machine literally brown, which is the banned sepia register, and the family boundaries stop reading so the four parts vanish. Do not retry a per-book palette without solving the family break first.
  • Two colours with the hatch cranked to 20% (dir-c.png). The control. It proves loudness alone is not the fix — it answers "too subtle" and nothing of "boilerplate". Kept as the evidence.

Method worth reusing

Judge a cover change as the whole shelf, rendered, at 96px — not one cover at full size. All three directions were built as contact sheets showing sixteen covers at 200px and 96px, and both rejections are visible only at that scale. data/covers/options/palette/build.py generates them; point it at new palettes.

Open questions

  • The ten built Instagram cards still carry the old two-colour scheme, so a card and its cover no longer match. cards-recolour is in the queue as todo.
  • design.md's "no third colour, ever" is dead, killed by the person it was written for. The replacement is tighter: four fields, one per part, never a fifth.
