Instagram cards — the first 10

What we decided

The card is a generator, not 10 hand-made files. data/cards/cards.json holds copy (number, field colour, headline with one <em> for the red word, kicker) and data/cards/build.py writes the 10 HTML files from one template. To change a card, edit the JSON and re-run. Never hand-edit card-NN.html — it is generated and will be overwritten.

Rendering is headless Chrome at --window-size=1080,1350. Google Fonts load over the network, so --virtual-time-budget=6000 is required or the type falls back to system sans and the render is silently wrong.

Layout, decided at the render: flat field (ink / field_blue, strictly alternating so the IG profile grid checkerboards), faint 40px grid at 10% opacity, top rail, then the signature numbered callout on a leader line pointing straight at the headline, then the headline at 90–122px. Type scale is by character count — short headlines get bigger, not the same size with more air. Bottom slack is intentional: it is the "generosity with white space" counterweight to the cold technical register.

Copy mix: 6 cards are hooks for The Dollar Empire, 4 carry the positioning (the promise, who it is for, how we write). Two weeks of weekday posting.

What we tried and abandoned

  • Vertically centring the headline block — first render put it dead centre with equal dead space above and below. It read as a quote graphic, not a cover. Anchored the block to the upper two-thirds instead, per docs/design.md.
  • Red on a whole phrase ("printing press", "half the news"). The audit caught it and it is right: rule 3 is one word, and on card 10 the red span wrapped across a line break, which reads as a rendering bug. One word only, and it must not straddle a wrap.

The audit

One Sonnet subagent, given docs/design.md and the renders, judged the 270px feed crop first. 5 defects, 2 blocking. All resolved — 4 fixed, 1 (card 01's one-word line) kept on purpose and logged. Full record: data/audits/cards-legibility.md.

Legibility at 270px, grid alternation and the square profile crop all passed on the first render, which is the part that usually fails. The floor rule in design.md did its job.

Open questions

  • Card 01 vs card 08 as the launch post. 01 is the strongest sentence; 08 states the three promises plainly. Untested either way.
  • Captions. The cards have no caption copy yet. Instagram captions carry the CTA to the bio link, so they are needed before Matt posts, not after.
  • Cards 11-20. The plan is 5 a week; 10 covers two weeks. The next batch should wait for real engagement data, per the plan's "replace borrowed guesses with real ones".
