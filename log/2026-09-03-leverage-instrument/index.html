Run 4 of the Surplus blurb eval fixed a broken leverage question and overturned rule 1 — blurbs should mention nothing modern at all.

Leverage: the instrument was broken, not the blurbs

What we decided

Run 3's own diagnosis was wrong and we corrected it. Run 3 closed saying two rules were fighting — rule 1 says gesture at the category instead of naming the instance, but a judge scores leverage 3 only when he names a present-day thing unprompted. Read properly, they do not fight. The question had a dead band: the judge was asked "name one present-day thing", named the one the blurb had just handed him, and honestly scored himself 2 because 2 is defined as "the example the blurb handed you". Gesture → capped at 2. Gesture at nothing → 1. A 3 was reachable only by accident, and run 3 produced none across 15 scores.

The fix: ask in two parts. (a) what does the blurb point at? (b) now name something different. Score (b) only. Re-ran leverage only on the same five run-3 candidates with the same three judges. Scores spread from flat ~2 to 1.67–3.00. The instrument works now.

The finding overturns hooks.md rule 1. Every leverage 3 came from a blurb whose part (a) was "none" — it mentioned nothing modern at all. Every blurb that reached for a modern hook, even a categorical one, capped at 2; judges gave the category back in new clothes and said so unprompted. Rule 1 was "gesture at the category, never name the instance". It is now "stay in the past to the last word, and let him reach." Rewritten in hooks.md.

We did not re-ship. S1, the staff-only-door blurb, is still live and still the best candidate: the only one with plain 3.00 and 2/3 on the $9 gate.

What we tried and abandoned

  • Cutting S1's modern last line to lift its leverage. Not done: that line is also the line two of three judges quoted for hype. The trade is real and unpriced. Logged in eval.md as the thing the next round must test — write both endings, let judges price it. Do not assume.
  • Another generation round. Matt has not given his verdict on the shipped blurb yet. Writing more drafts before he speaks is churn.

Open questions

  • Subject, not leverage, is now S1's ceiling. 2.00, all three judges, all scoring exactly 2. Everyone can say the book is about stored food; nobody can say it from the first two sentences. That is the next fix on this blurb.
  • The pass bar has never been met by anything. Is 3/3 on the $9 gate plus four criteria ≥2.3 achievable at all, or is the bar itself wrong? Three runs say nothing clears it. Worth testing the bar against a blurb from a book that actually sells before we chase it further.
  • Does the "stay in the past" rule survive contact with the news-led carousels, where the whole premise is starting from today's headline? Probably a format-specific exception, but nobody has checked.
