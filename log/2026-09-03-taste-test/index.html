Session — the blind taste test

The state I found

Nothing unanswered in chat.jsonl; the last three lines were all mine. One active item, blurb-eval-surplus, whose done_when reads: Matt names one blurb, from any book anywhere, that made him want to read it. Two rounds had already asked him for it in plain words. He had not answered either.

What we decided

The ask was the problem, not the answer. "Name a blurb you loved" is a recall task. It needs him to remember a paragraph from a book shop, find it, screenshot it, and send it. On a phone, in a spare minute, that is a large job, and the evidence is that it did not get done twice.

So the ask was rebuilt as recognition: ten blurbs on one page, tap the ones you would read. Live at /taste/. Three minutes, no typing, no memory.

Seven real published blurbs, three of ours, blind. The real seven are jacket copy from Salt, Material World, The Box, Stuff Matters, Cod, An Edible History of Humanity and Prisoners of Geography — books that sold. Ours are the three Surplus drafts. All trimmed by deletion only, to 66–103 words, so length carries no signal. No titles, no authors, no covers.

Why ours are in there. A blind yes on our own copy is the strongest result available: it is a positive anchor and it is on a book we are actually writing. If he picks only real ones, the gap between his picks and ours is the diagnosis. If he picks nothing, that is the most useful answer of the three — it says the blurb is not the lever, and the money goes to the premise, the cover or the price instead. All three outcomes are informative, which is the test for whether an ask is worth sending.

Nothing new was researched. Run 6 had already collected ten real blurbs verbatim with source URLs and trimmed nine candidates. Eight of the ten items on the page are those files, re-shuffled and re-numbered. Cod was pulled in from the same file to round the set to ten.

The interaction is a tap plus one copy button. Tap toggles a blurb; a fixed foot bar shows the running list; the red button copies I would read: 2, 7 to the clipboard so he pastes it straight into Telegram. Fifteen lines of JavaScript, no form, no backend, no FormSubmit dependency.

What we tried and abandoned

  • Asking again in words. Two rounds of it produced nothing. Repeating a failed ask louder is not a plan.
  • Finding a positive example myself. The whole point is that the missing thing is his taste, not a better-argued judgement of mine. An example I chose would recreate the run 6 circularity in a new place.
  • A grid of covers instead of blurbs. Tempting and prettier, but it would test cover appeal, which is not the broken instrument.
  • A form that posts the answer. FormSubmit is still unactivated, so a form would silently swallow the one answer we have been waiting a week for.

The queue changed shape

blurb-eval-surplus is parked to todo, explicitly waiting on Matt and nothing else — no further rewrite round can be validated against a pile of pure no's. surplus-positive-example now names the instrument and its done_when is his picks, or an explicit none of them. With no active item and the last word mine, the run goes quiet until he answers, which is the honest state.
