Source files: data/channels-social-video.md (9 channels, scored 2026-08-28) and data/channels-text-community.md (13 channels, scored 2026-08-28) — both now superseded by this note. Two scores were re-leveled to match a common standard; see "Normalisation" below.

Rubric (1–5 each, 30 max)

  1. Reach — reach to our reader: curious adults who read big-picture explainer history.
  2. Faceless — works with no human face, no personal brand.
  3. Sustain — an AI agent alone can keep it running (volume, format, no live human presence required).
  4. Cost — zero cost to run, free tools only.
  5. Evidence — evidence someone already wins here: a real account/number, URL, date.
  6. Speed — days to first signal; fast feedback scores high.

Evidence dates are all 2026-08-28 unless stated. "Live fetch" = URL opened directly. "Search snippet" = WebSearch reported it with a source URL but a direct fetch could not independently confirm it. not found is carried through as-is, never guessed.

Top 3

1. Instagram (Reels + carousels) — 28. Two real faceless comps live-fetched at 6.3M and 313K followers prove the exact format (faceless carousels/reels in this niche) works at multi-million scale, and it scores near-max on every axis except raw reach. Matt already holds the account, removing the one practical blocker (zero-to-account cold start) that slows every other channel.

2. TikTok — 27. The algorithm gives the fastest cold-start of any channel in the set (Speed 5/5) and a directly on-niche account (286.4K followers, 5M likes) proves the format works, even though that number is search-snippet rather than live-fetch confirmed. It loses a point to Instagram only on evidence-confirmation strength, not on format fit.

3. YouTube Shorts — 27. Rides the same live-fetched channel evidence as YouTube long-form (Simple History 5.1M subs, Kings and Generals 4.12M) but without the production load — it's a faster-feedback way to test the same explainer-history format before committing to long-form video.

Normalisation — scores adjusted

Two agents scored independently against the same rubric and drifted on two rows. Both are re-leveled here, not in the source files (which are kept as the original raw scoring record):

  • Reddit, Sustain: 3 → 2. Reddit's own verdict states the channel "works only if Matt is the one posting and replying" — an agent can draft but not sustain it, per Reddit's bot-posting ban. That is the same constraint that earned Facebook Groups a Sustain score of 2 in the social file ("an agent can draft, not sustain the relational work"). Scoring the same limitation a 3 in one file and a 2 in the other was inconsistent; leveled down to 2. Reddit's total moves from 27 to 26 but it stays the top text/community channel.
  • Hacker News, Evidence: 2 → 4. HN's evidence is genuinely strong — a 7-URL table of real point counts, live-fetched via the HN Algolia API, the same evidence-quality tier that earned Instagram a 5 and TikTok a 4 elsewhere. The original 2 conflated "wrong genre" (an HN tech/startup-audience problem, already captured by HN's Reach score of 2) with "no evidence found" (which isn't true — the evidence exists, it's just off-topic). Leveled up to 4, matching the standard used for other channels with confirmed live-fetched numbers regardless of niche fit. HN's total moves from 23 to 25.

No other scores were changed. Everything else in both source files held to a consistent standard on inspection — including some that looked drift-prone at first glance (e.g. TikTok's unconfirmed 286K-follower Evidence score of 4 vs. Substack's confirmed-but-small 1,000-subscriber Evidence score of 2): those reflect a real difference in the strength of the underlying proof, not inconsistent leveling, so they were left as scored.

Ban risk

Only the 13 text/community channels were researched for ban/policy risk this round; none of the 9 social/video channels were — that is a real gap in the social file, not a "no risk" finding, and is marked "Unresearched" above rather than implied safe.

Explicit policy found: - Amazon — HIGH. Anti-Manipulation Policy explicitly bans self-reviewing, incentivized reviews, review-selling; enforcement includes suspension, delisting, lawsuits. Automated request-a-review (1x/order, Seller Central) is a separate, permitted mechanism. https://www.amazon.com/gp/help/customer/display.html?nodeId=G8CXDFT9GLRRSV3G - Reddit — HIGH. Bot/automated posting banned outright; AI-assisted drafting with human review and posting is tolerated. 90/10 self-promo rule risks shadowban/ban. At least one subreddit (r/programming) has gone "AI-Free Zone" in 2026. https://aibrify.com/blog/ai-reddit-marketing-compliant, https://redship.io/blog/reddit-self-promotion-rules-2026, https://vucense.com/privacy-sovereignty/digital-independence/reddit-programming-ai-content-ban-2026/ - Goodreads — MODERATE. ToS bans spam/unsolicited commercial messages and identity misrepresentation. https://www.goodreads.com/about/terms - Medium — MODERATE. Username policy favors real names over brand/pseudonym accounts; AI-generated text is barred from Partner Program monetization (not a full platform ban). https://policy.medium.com/medium-username-policy-7054a77fb04f

Known pattern, not independently fetched this round: - Wikipedia referral — HIGH. Conflict-of-interest/self-citation policy; self-added citations are commonly reverted, repeat offenders banned.

Unresearched (flagged open, not assumed safe): Hacker News, Discord, niche history forums, Quora, Substack Notes, newsletter-swap communities, text-based book communities, and all 9 social/video channels (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, YouTube long-form, Threads, X/Twitter, Facebook Groups, Pinterest, LinkedIn).

None: SEO/own blog — no platform gatekeeper; only risk is a content-quality SEO penalty (current Google wording not independently confirmed).
