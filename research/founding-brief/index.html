The founding brief — what the business is, who it is for, and the constraints, from Matt's brain dump.

Brief

What we are building

Short, big-picture history books in the style of Yuval Noah Harari — one topic per book, not one book for all of history.

The thing Matt wants and cannot buy: history that skips dates, names and mnemonic detail, and instead explains why a thing mattered, how it changed the world, and how it fits the global picture — religions, mentalities, systems, human evolution. Harari does this, but there are only a few of his books, and each one covers everything at once.

Candidate series topics (Matt's own list): the Dollar Empire, the World Wars and how they handed the century to America, the British Empire, the Americas, religions.

First test topic: The Dollar Empire.

Why it can work

Matt is reader #1. He has the exact need, so he is also the quality check. If a book bores him, it ships to nobody.

Goal

Real income within 12 months. Not a hobby, not an audience-only play.

Who it is for

  1. People like Matt — curious adults ~25–45, tech/business minded, read non-fiction to understand the world, no patience for trivia.
  2. The self-improvement crowd — buy books to get smarter. Bigger, spends more, shallower.

Both are vast. Open task: find a smaller, more targetable niche inside them.

Rejected: hardcore history fans (Hardcore History, Fall of Civilizations). They already know the material and will judge us on detail we deliberately cut. Rejected: students and exam-takers. They want exactly the details we remove.

Market

English, global.

The order of work: distribution first

This is the sharpest decision in the brief.

Books are expensive to make well. So we do not write books until we know how they reach buyers. If distribution cannot be figured out, the books may not be worth the effort at all.

Best possible outcome: money or emails before a book exists — a presale, a waitlist, a landing page that converts. Then write.

Product shape — deliberately undecided

Open options, to be settled by validation, not by opinion: - EPUB/PDF books sold direct, or on Amazon and similar. - A website that IS the product: a chronological series you read and check off, like a curriculum where each book is a class and you graduate. Progress tracking, not gamification. - A subscription: new book each month. - One MVP book first, site later — or the site first as the validation device.

Book length is also open: short (1–2h read) or medium, depending on the collection. To be tested.

Brand and voice

  • Faceless. No Matt on camera, no Matt voice. AI voice or static visuals are fine.
  • Brand, no author. A publisher-style imprint name on the cover. No person, no pen-name persona.
  • We cannot say "reads like Harari" in marketing. Open task: find the honest way to signal the same promise.

Constraints

  • Agents do ~99% of the work. Matt oversees and does quality control on the books. Everything must be designed to run without him.
  • No paid ads until something proves it converts organically.
  • No account access. Claude drafts; Matt posts, publishes, and handles money.
  • Never fake human authorship. No invented credentials, no claiming a real person wrote it.
  • No monthly tool bills. Free tiers and one-off costs only.
  • Money: time is the budget. Small spend is possible, but only against evidence it is clearly worth it.

Open questions (the real work)

  1. Distribution. What is the simplest organic channel that reaches ICP 1 and 2 — Instagram posts, short video, TikTok, YouTube, newsletter, Reddit, something else? Which can a faceless agent-run operation actually sustain?
  2. Niche. Is there a tighter segment than "curious adults" we can own?
  3. Validation. What is the cheapest test that produces emails or presales before a book exists?
  4. Competition. What already exists — books, apps, course-style sites, newsletters — and what do comparable products earn?
  5. Positioning. How do we promise "Harari for one topic" without saying it?
  6. Format and price. Direct sale vs Amazon vs subscription; short vs medium.
