What repeats across nearly all of them

  1. Two columns on mobile, always. Every real grid (not the Stripe outlier) uses exactly 2 columns at 390px. Nobody tries 1 or 3.
  2. The blurb never lives in the grid. Every site keeps the grid to cover + at most title/author/price, and pushes the blurb to a click-through detail page. A grid with visible blurbs does not appear anywhere in this set.
  3. Gap is small relative to cover width — roughly 8–20% of cover width, not a wide gutter. Standard Ebooks' 44px gap is the outlier (also has 44px side padding, so the grid reads as heavily inset, deliberate breathing room for a huge catalogue).
  4. True book-proportioned covers (2:3, ~0.63–0.67 ratio) read as more "real" than square-cropped ones. Standard Ebooks and NYRB, the two with true book ratio, look most like a bookshelf; Persephone's square crop and Fitzcarraldo's 3D render read more "product photo."
  5. Side padding on mobile is small — 15–24px on the tightest, most confident layouts (NYRB, Fitzcarraldo); Standard Ebooks' 44px is unusually generous and reads more spacious/institutional, matching its huge catalogue.
  6. Desktop max-width sits around 1200–1450px, never full-bleed to the viewport edge — always some centred column even on a wide screen.
  7. Nothing browsable beyond the grid itself for 16-ish items. None of these sites add sticky filters or section headers until the catalogue is in the hundreds (Standard Ebooks, with thousands of titles, is the only one with heavy filter UI). At our scale (16 covers) a plain grid, optionally with light section headers, is what real sites do — filter chrome would be over-building.

Recommended spec for our 16-cover /covers/ shelf

Base pattern: NYRB Classics, because it has the tightest, most confident mobile layout (true 2:3 covers, minimal padding, no wasted chrome) and it is the closest match to what we already have — type-only cover art with no photography to crop.

  • Columns: 2 at mobile (≤600px), 4 at desktop (≥1000px). No 3-column tier — none of the reference sites use one, and it fights a 2:3 cover ratio.
  • Cover size: keep our existing 2:3 portrait ratio. Cover width = (container width − side padding×2 − gap) / 2 on mobile; let it scale fluidly, don't fix px.
  • Gap: 12px between covers, both axes. (Between NYRB's 10px and Fitzcarraldo's 16px — tight, not a wide gutter.)
  • Side padding on mobile: 16px (matches NYRB's 15px; anchors to our existing text-column grid per design.md rule 7).
  • Page max-width on desktop: 1200px, centred.
  • Text under the cover: title only. No author line (we're a faceless imprint, no author persona — positioning.md), no price on this page (price lives on the offer, not the coming-soon shelf).
  • Blurb: not in the grid, at any width. It lives one tap away — tapping/clicking a cover opens the blurb (inline expand under that cover, or a lightbox/detail view — either is fine, but the grid itself must stay cover+title only, per every reference site).
  • Grouping: keep our existing four-part grouping from catalogue.md, rendered as a plain section heading above each group of 4 covers — no filters, no sort, no sticky controls. At 16 items that chrome is unearned; add it only if the catalogue grows past ~50.

This is a direct, minimal change from what's on the site now: keep the part-grouping and type-only covers we already built, tighten the grid to 2/4 columns with a small fixed gap and small side padding, drop any per-cover blurb text from the grid, and move the blurb behind a tap.
