Amazon KDP and format rules for Engine Room Press

1. Ebook royalty: 35% vs 70%

35% 70%
Price range Any price US $2.99–$12.99 (raised from $9.99 on 7 Jul 2026); UK £1.77–£12.99; EU €2.69–€12.99; CA $2.99–$12.99; AU $3.99–$15.99
Delivery cost deduction None Yes, charged per MB of file size
Territory Any KDP territory Only "70% territories" listed on Amazon's Pricing Page; outside those, royalty drops to 35% even if enrolled in the 70% plan
  • Delivery cost: $0.15/MB in the US tier by some third-party summaries; Amazon's own average cited is ~$0.06/unit sold, varying by file size and territory (UK ~£0.10/MB, EU ~€0.12/MB per third-party summary — not independently confirmed on Amazon's own page, treat as approximate).
  • Formula (Amazon's own wording): "70% of list price without VAT, less delivery costs, for units sold in 70% territories; 35% of list price without VAT for units sold outside 70% territories."
  • Source: KDP eBook Royalty Rates and Territories, KDP eBook List Price Requirements, builtwritten.com on the July 2026 cap change

For a ~100-page / $2.99–$4.99 history ebook, this comfortably sits in the 70% band on all major markets.

2. Box sets / bundles

  • Allowed: a single ASIN containing the content of two or more individual titles, sold as one ebook product ("boxed/bundle edition"). Source: KDP Boxed and Bundle Edition Guidelines.
  • You must clearly disclose in the title/description that the product is a multi-book bundle, and disclose any content variations (revised, expanded, abridged, previously published).
  • Not allowed: reordering the same set of books into a "different" box set to game the catalogue (Amazon explicitly bans e.g. having both "Books 1–4" and "Books 2,3,4,1" as separate box sets). Source: kboards discussion citing KDP support — community-sourced, not an official Amazon page; treat as directionally reliable, not verbatim policy text.
  • Component books being also sold separately: Amazon's official guideline page does not explicitly bar or require this either way — not found as an explicit written rule. Common practice (and no reported enforcement issue) is that individual titles remain on sale alongside the bundle.
  • Box sets are eBook-only; KDP does not support bundled/boxed paperback editions.
  • Publishing a box set before the individual books exist: not found as an explicit prohibition on Amazon's own guidelines page. One community source states you do not need the full series complete before creating a box set/series. Treat with caution — this is not confirmed on an official Amazon page, and a bundle with placeholder or non-existent component titles risks running into Amazon's general content-accuracy and pre-order rules.
  • Note: KDP's own current guidance nudges toward not using bundles at all — "For eBooks, KDP encourages prioritizing individual titles linked to a series for better discoverability and reader flexibility" (from the same official guidelines page).

3. KDP Series pages

  • What it does: creates one dedicated series landing page listing every book in reading order, linked from each book's own product page via a clickable series name.
  • Discovery benefit: yes — it's Amazon's primary "customers who bought book 1, buy book 2" surface; a live series page appears once at least two titles in the series are live.
  • Requirements: all books published through KDP; identical series name (case-sensitive, exact match) in the Series Name metadata field across every book; series name + number must also appear on the cover, in the Look Inside interior, or in the product description; books must be full-length (this excludes prequels/novellas/short pieces from qualifying, per current guidance) and available for purchase.
  • Source: KDP Start a Book Series
  • Caveat: the "full-length only" claim comes from a third-party summary (scribecount.com), not verified verbatim on Amazon's own page — worth re-checking before relying on it for a series of short (~100-page) history ebooks, since Engine Room Press's whole catalogue may not qualify as "full-length."

4. Reviews

  • A review without a sale: allowed by Amazon's mechanics (anyone can review a product), but it will not carry the "Verified Purchase" badge, and non-verified reviews carry less weight in star-rating and sorting algorithms. Source: wiserreview.com summary of Amazon review policy
  • Verified Purchase mechanics: badge requires the reviewer bought the item on Amazon.com; reviewer must generally have spent at least $50 on Amazon in the past 12 months via a valid card to leave any review at all (this $50 threshold is a widely repeated figure — not independently confirmed on an official Amazon policy page in this research pass).
  • Review swaps: explicitly banned — "review-for-product" services and reciprocal review swaps between authors/sellers are prohibited and are described as a fast route to listing removal / account action.
  • Incentivized reviews: banned outright except through Amazon Vine (Amazon-run, Amazon-selected, Vine Voice badge). A free copy, refund, or gift in exchange for a review violates policy regardless of whether a "may be biased" disclosure is included.
  • Reviews of a free KDP Select promo download: not found — could not confirm via an official Amazon source whether reviews from readers who got the book via a KDP Select free-promo day are treated differently (they would not carry Verified Purchase either way, since no payment occurred).
  • Realistic expectation for an unknown imprint: no hard number found. Indie-publishing community consensus (not an Amazon-sourced figure) is that new/unknown authors typically see reviews trickle in from a very small fraction of readers (often cited informally as under 1%), and building past the first handful of reviews takes real distribution — no citable, current, Amazon-sourced statistic exists for this; treat any specific percentage as folklore, not fact.
  • Sources: wiserreview.com — Incentivized reviews 2026, sellersprite.com — Amazon Review Manipulation Suspensions 2026

5. KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited

  • Enrollment is in fixed 90-day terms, auto-renewing unless you opt out.
  • Exclusivity forbids: selling, lending, or giving away the enrolled ebook in digital form anywhere else during the term — including on your own website/store. This is a real constraint for a publisher that wants to sell direct.
  • Exception: up to 10% of the text may be posted elsewhere (e.g. your own site) as a promotional excerpt/sample.
  • Exclusivity applies to the ebook edition only — a paperback/print edition of the same book can be sold anywhere (IngramSpark, your own site, etc.) simultaneously with ebook Select enrollment.
  • KENP payout: paid from a monthly KDP Select Global Fund, divided by total Kindle Edition Normalized Pages read platform-wide; the rate is not fixed and moves monthly. Reported 2026 monthly rates: ~$0.0038–$0.0052 per page across the year, with July 2026 at roughly $0.0042/page and the July fund size around $67.6M. These are third-party-aggregated figures (not an official Amazon-published rate); treat as approximate and expect monthly variation.
  • Sources: scribecount.com — KDP Select operational guide, KDP Terms and Conditions, profitable.app — KENP rate tracker, Aug 2026, writtenwordmedia.com — KDP Global Fund payouts

6. Paperback via KDP print-on-demand

  • Work required: choose a trim size (6"x9" is the common default for narrative non-fiction), build the interior as a print-ready PDF with correct margins, produce a single cover-spread PDF/image (front + spine + back) with spine width calculated from final page count and paper type, and add 0.125" (3.2mm) bleed on any page/cover element that runs to the edge.
  • Amazon provides an official Cover Calculator that outputs exact spine width and full cover dimensions once you enter trim size, page count, and paper type — this removes most of the manual math.
  • Minimum page count: 24 pages (6"x9" regular trim supports 24–828 pages). Source: KDP Paperback Submission Guidelines.
  • A ~20,000-word / ~100-page book sits comfortably in the "24–110 pages" printing-cost band for black-ink, regular-trim paperbacks in the US: fixed printing cost $2.30/copy, no per-page charge below 110 pages. Source: KDP Paperback Printing Cost (figure via third-party summary of that page, cambric.pub).
  • Royalty rate: since June 2025, print books priced under $9.99 earn 50% royalty on list price minus printing cost; books priced $9.99 or above earn 60%.
  • Minimum viable list price: the list price must cover the printing cost out of the author's royalty share — Amazon will not let you set a price where the resulting royalty is negative. For a $2.30 printing cost at the 50% band, break-even list price is roughly $4.60 (2.30 / 0.50); Amazon's KDP dashboard calculates and enforces the actual minimum at title-setup time, and it may run a little above pure break-even. Not independently confirmed as an exact Amazon-stated minimum — treat $4.60 as an estimate, verify against the live KDP pricing tool at setup.
  • Example royalty math at $9.99 (crossing into the 60% band): $9.99 × 0.60 − $2.30 printing cost ≈ $3.69/copy. Below $9.99 the 50% rate applies instead.
  • Time cost for a first-timer: no Amazon-published estimate. Not found as a hard figure; general indie-publishing accounts describe interior formatting + cover-spread setup + proof ordering as a multi-day task the first time (design tool learning curve, one physical proof-and-review cycle adds shipping time), but no citable current source gives a specific hour count.
  • Does a paperback edition help the ebook's ranking or credibility? Each format (ebook, paperback, hardcover) has its own independent Amazon Best Sellers Rank — paperback sales do not feed into or boost the ebook's rank, and vice versa. Source: community/industry summary, no ranking-mechanics detail published officially by Amazon on this specific point. A paperback may still help overall legitimacy/credibility of an unknown imprint (readers seeing a "real" print edition), but there's no evidence of a direct ranking or algorithmic benefit to the ebook — not found as a documented mechanism.

7. Preorders and selling an unwritten book

Amazon KDP preorder rules

  • Maximum lead time: preorders can be set up to 1 year before the release date. Source: rogerpacker.com on the KDP preorder lead-time extension, KDP — Kindle eBook Pre-Order
  • Final manuscript must be uploaded at least 72 hours (3 days) before the scheduled release date.
  • Penalty for missing the delivery deadline: the preorder is cancelled, all customers who preordered are notified of the cancellation, and the publishing account loses preorder privileges for one year.
  • One-time flexibility: a single release-date delay of up to 30 days is allowed without penalty; a second delay (or missing the deadline outright) triggers the one-year preorder ban.
  • Sources: KDP — Pre-order statuses, KDP — Delay or cancel a pre-order release

Direct-sale side: taking money for an ebook that doesn't exist yet (EU consumer law)

High level, not legal advice: - Under the EU Consumer Rights Directive, consumers generally have a 14-day right of withdrawal on distance/online purchases, including digital content. - For digital content, a trader can only get the customer to waive that 14-day right if: (a) the customer gives explicit prior consent to immediate performance, and (b) explicitly acknowledges losing the withdrawal right once delivery/download starts. If a trader doesn't implement this consent flow correctly, the withdrawal right survives — and if the required withdrawal information wasn't given at all, the window can extend up to a year. - Practical read for a "buy now, book delivered later" preorder model: since the digital content (the finished ebook) has not been delivered yet, the "delivery has started" exception doesn't apply — the 14-day withdrawal right almost certainly still applies in full up to and after the point of actual delivery, meaning a customer who prepays for an unwritten book can very likely demand a refund within 14 days of either the purchase or the eventual delivery, subject to how the specific consent/notice was worded. - Separately, EU digital-content rules also carry a broader "conformity" guarantee (content must work as described; remedies include repair, replacement, price reduction, or full refund if it doesn't). - Sources (informational, not legal advice): europe-consommateurs.eu — Right of withdrawal, key-g.com — EU digital goods withdrawal rights, case law 2026, osborneclarke.com — Digital platforms introducing 14-day refund policies - Takeaway for Engine Room Press: taking prepayment directly (outside Amazon) for a book that isn't finished carries real EU refund-exposure risk unless the checkout flow handles consent/withdrawal-waiver correctly — this is a genuine open legal question, not a settled "just don't do it" or "it's fine," and would benefit from actual legal advice before building a direct-preorder funnel.
