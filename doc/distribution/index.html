Distribution

The decision

One channel: Instagram. Image-card carousels, five a week, one direct bio link to an email capture page.

TikTok and YouTube Shorts are parked until Instagram works. Not rejected — parked. A one-person-plus-agent operation running three platforms on day one runs none of them well, and the graveyard is full of solo operators who stopped quietly from burnout.

Why Instagram and not the other two, when all three scored within one point of each other:

  1. It is the only one of the three with a proven off-platform funnel in our niche. @geopolitical.guy (377K followers) links straight from bio to a Substack newsletter, one click, no linktree. Among every TikTok winner researched, not one had a confirmed route off-platform. TikTok is awareness. Instagram is the only one that visibly collects emails.
  2. The format is the cheapest thing an agent can make. Static text cards. No video render, no voice, no footage licensing. An agent produces a week's batch in one pass.
  3. The faceless proof is strongest here. Live-fetched faceless accounts in this niche run from 74.8K to 6.3M followers.
  4. Matt already holds the account. Zero cold start on the one thing an agent cannot do for him.

What we actually post

Five image-card posts a week, one per weekday. Not Reels. Static cards with a consistent template — one font system, one colour system, one look — because the surviving faceless winners look hand-made, not templated-at-volume. The agent drafts five posts with two or three alternate hook lines each; Matt look-checks and posts.

The content unit is one idea from the book's argument, told whole. Not trivia, not "10 facts." Every card should leave the reader understanding something they did not understand before, and should be recognisably from the same mind as the last one.

Full operating detail, hooks, and comps: docs/notes/channel-deepdive.md.

The funnel arithmetic

The chain from stranger to first euro:

Instagram reach → profile visit → bio-link click → email signup → book sale

The product at the end is a $4.99 KDP ebook, netting about $3.44 after Amazon's 70% royalty and delivery fee. First euro = first sale.

Assumptions, with their sources and honesty ratings:

Step Rate assumed Where it comes from Honesty
Profile visit → bio-link click 3% Industry-standard link-in-bio click-through band. No first-party figure for our niche exists. LOW — this is the weakest number in the chain.
Bio-link click → email signup 30% A dedicated single-purpose capture page, nothing else on it. LOW-MEDIUM
Email subscriber → buyer of a $4.99 book 5% Below the ~3-10% band typical for a warm list buying a low-priced first product. Chosen at the low end on purpose. LOW

Multiplied through: one paying stranger needs roughly 450 profile visits.

450 visits × 3% = 13.5 clicks → × 30% = 4 emails → × 5% = 0.2 sales. So ~450 visits buys about one-fifth of a sale; five weeks of that cadence buys one. Put plainly: at five posts a week with a small account, expect the first paying stranger somewhere in month two or three, not week one.

That is the honest number and it is deliberately unflattering. Every rate above is a guess borrowed from outside our niche, because no first-party figure exists — see the caveat below.

What has to be true for this to work

  1. The cards get distribution at all. A faceless account with zero followers is at the mercy of Instagram's recommendation of the post itself. If posts do not reach non-followers, nothing downstream matters.
  2. The bio link is one click to one page. No linktree, no menu. The single most copyable thing in the whole research set.
  3. The email capture page exists before the first post. Posting without it wastes every visit. This is the dependency that makes landing urgent.
  4. Matt posts consistently. Five a week, every week. The agent removes every excuse not to — batches ready, hooks pre-written, nothing left to decide.
  5. There is something to buy. Emails accumulate for nothing if book one never ships.

The kill tripwire

Written now, before we are emotionally invested:

By day 60 — 40 posts in — if the account is under 200 followers AND has produced fewer than 25 email signups, Instagram is wrong for us and we stop.

Two failure modes and what each means:

  • Followers grow, emails do not → the content works, the funnel is broken. Fix the bio link and the capture page. Do not change the content.
  • Neither grows → the format or the topic is wrong on this platform. Switch to the parked channel (TikTok, on the POV/fact-hook format, which the research says is what actually wins there).

The tripwire exists because every dead operator in the graveyard stopped quietly, with no decision point written down. This is the decision point.

What we are NOT doing, and why

  • Not Reddit, despite scoring the highest reach of any channel. Bot posting is banned outright and only a human posting and replying makes it work. That puts the load on Matt, and he is the constraint.
  • Not Amazon's internal search first. Highest buyer intent of anything, but it only activates once a book exists. Sequenced last, not never.
  • Not a paid newsletter. Slowest path to a first sale of every model, and needs a list we do not have.
  • Not three platforms at once. See above.
  • Not paid ads. Standing constraint until something converts organically.

The weakness in this document

Every conversion rate above is borrowed, not measured. No first-party figure exists for an account like ours, in our niche, at our size — we looked and did not find one. The funnel arithmetic is therefore a plan for what to measure, not a forecast to trust. The first 60 days replace these three numbers with real ones, and the plan gets rebuilt on those.
