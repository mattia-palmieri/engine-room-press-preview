The Essential Sixteen — sixteen short books, one force each, in four parts (Tools, Communication, Stories, Power), with the reading order, the publishing order, and why sixteen.

The catalogue

Sixteen books. Four parts, four books each. One force per book.

This is the whole "essential" shelf — the machinery a reader needs before the news makes sense. It is deliberately finite. When the sixteen are written, the imprint has a complete product, not an endless feed.

Every title passes the filter from positioning.md: it names a force, and it can finish the sentence "this is how X let humans do something they could not do before." No periods. No events. No people.

Why sixteen

Three reasons, and they agree:

  1. The price ladder sets it. Matt's numbers are $9 a book and a $69 bundle marked from ~$149. $149 ÷ $9 ≈ 16.5. Sixteen books priced separately is $144. The shelf is the bundle, and the bundle only holds together at this size. (Final list price is offer.md's call — flagging the arithmetic here.)
  2. Four is the number of forces. positioning.md names four levers: tools, communication, stories, power. Four books each covers a lever properly without padding it.
  3. It is writable. Sixteen short books is a year of shipping at a book every three weeks. Twenty-five is a shelf nobody finishes writing or reading.

Books that did not make the sixteen are not dead — they are Shelf Two at the bottom, and they are where we go after the essentials sell.

Part I — TOOLS

What humans could suddenly do.

1. The Surplus — how food you don't eat today invented kings, taxes and cities. Reader gets: every hierarchy on earth starts with somebody storing grain.

2. The Engine — how burning coal broke the ceiling on what muscles could lift. Reader gets: why "growth every year" is a two-hundred-year-old idea, not a law.

3. The Barrel — how oil turned energy into something you must ship, and therefore guard. Reader gets: why a strait most people cannot find on a map moves the price of everything.

4. The Machine — how a device that copies thinking is doing to offices what the engine did to muscles. Reader gets: a way to read the AI argument that is not hype or panic.

Part II — COMMUNICATION

How large a group of strangers can work as one.

5. Writing — how memory outside a human head made the state possible. Reader gets: bureaucracy was the killer app, not literature.

6. Print — how copies made without permission broke the monopoly on truth. Reader gets: the Reformation and the newspaper are the same machine running twice.

7. The Wire — how killing the delay between here and there synchronised markets, armies and empires. Reader gets: "real time" is an invention, and it has costs.

8. The Feed — how a copy machine that ranks what you see turned attention into the scarce resource. Reader gets: why nobody agrees on the facts any more.

Part III — STORIES

What lets strangers who will never meet cooperate.

9. Money — how a shared fiction about debt became the thing everyone spends their life chasing. Reader gets: money is a promise, and who gets to make the promise.

10. Gods — how a story nobody can check moved millions of people the same way. Reader gets: religion as cooperation technology, judged on what it did, not on whether it is true.

11. The Nation — how a very recent invention came to feel eternal. Reader gets: why borders, passports and "our people" are younger than the piano.

12. The Company — how we invented a person made of paper that never dies and cannot be jailed. Reader gets: limited liability is the most underrated idea in the modern world.

Part IV — POWER

What happens when the levers stack.

13. The Map — how straight lines drawn by strangers became the borders people now die for. Reader gets: most of today's wars are an argument with a hundred-year-old pencil.

14. The Chokepoint — how a handful of narrow places decide the price of everything. Reader gets: Hormuz, Suez, Malacca, Panama — what actually happens when one closes.

15. The Dollar — how one country's money became everyone's money, and what that buys it. Reader gets: sanctions, reserves and "the exorbitant privilege" in plain words.

16. The Bomb — how a weapon too big to use rewrote what powerful countries do instead of fighting. Reader gets: why the last eighty years look the way they do.

Two different orders, and do not confuse them

Reading order is 1 → 16, and it is the curriculum. Each part is built on the one before: tools make surplus, surplus needs records, records need stories to scale, and the stories stack into power. This is what we sell in the bundle and what "read them in order" means on the site.

Publishing order is different, and it is set by demand, not by logic. We ship the books that a stranger already wants to buy, then backfill. Recommended first five:

# Book Why it goes first
1 Money The most universal "how does this even work" in the catalogue, and the gateway drug to book 15.
2 The Chokepoint Live in the news right now (Hormuz). The news-led Instagram entry needs a book to land on.
3 The Nation Explains the single most reported subject on earth: borders, migration, "our people".
4 The Feed The reader is holding the product while reading the book. Highest share rate.
5 The Dollar Sells to everyone who finished Money. First real upsell.

The other eleven follow in reading order.

What is not proved

positioning.md flags it and it applies hardest here: every search-volume tool was blocked, so this order is judgement, not data. The presale queue item exists to fix exactly this — put all sixteen on the site as coming-soon with a one-click "tell me when this one is out", and let the clicks reorder the list. The catalogue is the hypothesis; the waitlist is the test.
