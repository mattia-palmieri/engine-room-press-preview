Design system — Engine Room Press

The system is "Technical Drawing": the page is an engineering cutaway of an idea. Thin measured rules, a faint grid, and numbered callouts that label real parts of the argument. It comes straight from the name and the promise — hidden machinery, shown.

Four surfaces, one system, different volume: reading = quiet · book cover = measured · landing = spoken · Instagram card = shouted. Same tokens throughout. Loudness is carried by scale and flat colour fields, never by new fonts or new colours.

Tokens

color:
  paper:        "#F7F6F1"   # default canvas. Near-white, cool. NOT parchment.
  paper_white:  "#FFFFFF"   # component fields, form inputs
  ink:          "#14171A"   # all body and display text; also a flat field on cards
  signal:       "#C1440E"   # oxide red. Callout tags, one word of emphasis, buttons.
  # THE FOUR FIELDS — one per part of the catalogue, and never a fifth. Flat
  # fields for covers and cards only; none is ever a text colour on paper.
  field_iron:   "#14171A"   # Part I   Tools          — iron black (same as ink)
  field_blue:   "#173B4E"   # Part II  Communication  — steel blue
  field_copper: "#17342A"   # Part III Stories        — oxidised copper
  field_aub:    "#251830"   # Part IV  Power          — aubergine

  rule_faint:   "#E4E2D8"   # the 40px measured grid

type:

  headline: { family: "Space Grotesk",  weight: 500, use: "section headings" }
  body:     { family: "Newsreader",     weight: 400, use: "everything a reader reads" }
  label:    { family: "IBM Plex Mono",  weight: 500, use: "callouts, figure numbers,
                                                captions, buttons; UPPERCASE, 0.12em" }

grid:     { unit: 40, rule_weight: 1 }
radius:   0            # nothing is rounded. Ever.
spacing:  [8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96]

Why these fonts. Space Grotesk is mechanical and modern without going cold-SaaS. Newsreader does the actual reading and keeps the thing a book, not an app. Plex Mono is the annotation voice — it is what makes a number read as measured rather than decorative. No Inter, no Helvetica, no Playfair, no decorative serif capitals.

Rules

  1. The signature device is the numbered callout. 01 ──────── One idea per book. A tag in signal red, a leader line, a label. It must always point at something real — a promise, a chapter, a part of the argument. The moment it points at nothing it is ornament and it is wrong.
  2. Everything is numbered. Sections are FIG. 01, cards are NO. 04, books are 01. The numbering is a real system, not a look.
  3. Signal red is rationed. Callout tags, one button, at most one emphasised word per surface. Never a background on paper. Never two red things competing.
  4. Nothing is rounded and nothing floats. No border-radius, no drop shadows, no cards with soft edges. Structure is hairlines and flat fields.
  5. Body copy is always the serif. If a paragraph is set in the grotesk, it is wrong.
  6. No gradient, no emoji, no icon triples, no stock photography, no faces.
  7. The grid is anchored to the text, never to the viewport. On paper surfaces the grid is vertical lines only — horizontal lines cross every line of type at a random height and that is exactly what reads as messy. The lines start at the text column's left edge and repeat every 40px, so the column's own edges always fall on a line. On the card the full grid stays; the cover carries the section hatch instead (see the cover surface below). On both, the padding is a whole number of modules: card 80/80/70 on a 40px module, cover 100/100/110 on a 50px module — chosen so the top and bottom content edges both land on a line despite the 1350 and 2560 canvas heights. Every rule, tag and rail then sits on the drawing, not near it.
  8. The banned register, restated because it is the whole point: parchment, sepia, gold, marble, engraved maps, dark academia. The reader bounced off that at school.
  9. The phone is the design width, and the numbers are copied, not invented. Every surface is judged at 390px first and 1440px second. Five working book shops — Fitzcarraldo, Standard Ebooks, Persephone, Verso, NYRB Classics — were measured live at both widths on 2026-09-03 and they agree on all five points below, so we follow them rather than guess (notes/book-site-teardown.md): - Two cover columns on a phone, four above 1000px. Never one, never three. - A blurb is never in a grid. It is one tap away, behind a :target panel — a bottom sheet on a phone, a centred box on a desktop. Putting the blurb in the grid is what opened an empty block under all sixteen covers on the old shelf. - Under a cover: the title, and almost nothing else. - Gap 12px between covers. Page padding 16px on a phone, 32px above 700px. - The page stops at 1200px. Wider and the covers stop being a shelf. A cover must also survive at 96px — that is feed and thumbnail size, and it is where a surface texture is decided, not at full size.

Surface: landing page (spoken)

Paper canvas with the faint 40px grid visible. Hero headline in display grotesk, three to four lines, left-aligned, ranged left on the grid. Subline in serif. The three promises are a parts list — numbered callouts on dashed leader lines. One email capture: a bordered white field block, mono label above, ink button with mono uppercase text. No navigation, no footer menu, no linktree, one action on the page.

Surface: Instagram card (shouted) — 1080 × 1350

The tie-break surface: Instagram is our only channel, so the card must win at 25% size in a scrolling feed.

  • Flat field, not paper. Full-bleed, one of the four part fields.
  • The carousel colour rule — colour carries meaning, not rhythm. A carousel is one post, so it is one colour: slides 1 to n-1 carry the field colour of the book's part — the same four fields as the covers, so a card and its cover are obviously the same object. The last slide flips to field_blue, or to ink if the book is already a Part II book, because the last slide is the ask and it should announce itself before a word is read. Brand cards with no book behind them are always ink. News carousels obey the same rule, taking the colour of the book they land on: Hormuz lands on The Barrel (Part I), so slides 1 and 2 are ink and slide 3, the ask, flips to field_blue. Alternating colour slide by slide is banned — it looked like rhythm and meant nothing. The ten built cards still carry the old two-colour scheme and are wrong until they are regenerated (cards-recolour in the queue).
  • Headline: display grotesk, 76–96px, max 14 words, filling the upper two-thirds. If it needs more than 14 words it is not a card, it is a caption.
  • One word may take signal red. One only.
  • Top rail: ENGINE ROOM PRESS left, NO. 04 right, mono 24px, letterspaced.
  • Bottom rail: hairline, then the wordmark. Nothing else.
  • The legibility floor: shrink the card to 270px wide. If the headline is not readable, the type is too small. Redo it, do not ship it.

Surface: book cover (measured) — 1600 × 2560

Sixteen covers must read as one imprint at Amazon thumbnail size (~160px). So the cover is pure type on a flat field — no illustration, no photograph, no bespoke art per title. The series reference is Fitzcarraldo and Penguin Great Ideas: identical construction, the title is the only variable.

  • One rule above all: the title is the cover. Nothing on the artwork may compete with it. The cover carries the title and one footer mark — nothing else. Series name, part, "no. 9 of 16", the one-line deck and the coming-soon state all belong on the page around the cover, never on it.
  • Four materials: one field colour and one hatch per part, and never a fifth. Each part is a different material cut open, in the real ISO 128 drafting hatches, so a reader can tell a Tools book from a Power book before reading a word. Pitch 24px, line weight 2px, at full 1600px size:
Part Field Material Hatch Opacity
I Tools #14171A iron black steel even 45° section hatch 24%
II Communication #173B4E steel blue glass alternating long/short 45° 14%
III Stories #17342A oxidised copper weave cross-hatch both ways 13%
IV Power #251830 aubergine concrete stipple grid + sparse strokes 14%

Ink carries a heavier hand than the rest because a white hairline on near-black has the least apparent contrast of the four fields. Match the rendered weight, not the number. The 50px module still governs the 100/100/110 padding. - A texture is decided at 96px, never at full size. Render the whole shelf at 96px and look at the PNG. Two schemes died here: a flat 50px grid that vanished into a slab, then a 7.5% hatch Matt called too subtle. Loudness alone is not the fix either — a two-colour shelf with the hatch cranked to 20% was built and rejected, and it is why the answer had to be colour and texture together (data/covers/options/palette/verdict.md). - Title: display grotesk, uppercase, ranged left, sized to fill the 1400px column — no word may cross it. Roughly 227px for CHOKEPOINT, up to a 420px cap for a short word. Every title is one word, so every cover reads as one big word. A leading THE is set at 0.30em in muted paper, on its own line above. - The title block sits at roughly 47% height, air above and below. Do not centre it and do not top-anchor it: both were tried and both read as an empty page. - The one footer mark: a hairline, then the red signal tag with the book number on the left and ENGINE ROOM PRESS in mono 32px on the right. One line, bottom of the cover. That is the entire remaining furniture. - The legibility floor: shrink the cover to 160px wide. If the title is not readable, the type is too small. At 160px the footer is deliberately illegible — it is a mark, not information.

Killed 2026-09-01 (Matt counted six labels fighting one title): the top rail (ENGINE ROOM PRESS / NO. 09 / 16), the numbered callout with PART III · STORIES, the Newsreader deck, and the bottom rail THE ESSENTIAL SIXTEEN / COMING SOON. The page that shows the shelf must carry the part grouping and the state instead.

Logo

The wordmark is ENGINE ROOM PRESS in IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, 0.16em tracking, ink on paper or paper on a flat field. No mark, no icon, no monogram. It comes last on purpose and it stays plain.

How this was chosen

Three directions were built with identical copy and judged on both surfaces at once (data/design/direction-{a,b,c}.html, screenshots alongside).

  • B, "The Ledger" — the best-looking landing page of the three, a genuinely handsome editorial serif. Rejected on the card. Its ruled-row language goes quiet exactly where we need volume, and it reads as a finance newsletter. Instagram is our only channel; a weak card is disqualifying no matter how good the page is.
  • C, "The Plate" — the loudest card and the best stopping power, but the giant numeral sat on top of the headline and ate it, and the device is decoration unless it points at a real number. Not rejected — absorbed. C is what taught us the card must be a flat colour field with type set far bigger than feels comfortable.
  • A, "Technical Drawing" — wins. Its device is the only one of the three that comes from the business's own story rather than from a genre, and it works at both volumes.

So the system is A's construction, run at C's volume on the card, with C's blue kept as a second field colour. A hybrid assembled from validated pieces, which is a proper end state and not a compromise.

The risk we are carrying

The technical register can read cold. Newsreader in the body and real generosity with white space are the counterweight. If a reader ever calls the page "clinical", the fix is more serif and more air — not a warmer colour, and never a slide back towards parchment.
