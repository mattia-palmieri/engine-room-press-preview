The 30-day plan to the first paying stranger — week 1 sets up the funnel, weeks 2-4 post five Instagram cards a week while book one gets written, and Matt's own jobs are listed day by day.

The 30-day plan

Day 1, in one line

Matt buys the domain and makes the email capture page live. The agent posts nothing until that page exists.

What we are aiming at

One paying stranger. Not a launch, not an audience, not a brand. One person we have never met, who pays $4.99 for a book.

Honest expectation, from the funnel arithmetic: the first sale probably lands in month two or three, not inside these 30 days. What these 30 days must produce is the machine that makes it possible — a live capture page, 20 posts, a real follower count, real email signups, and book one written. If the numbers at day 30 are on track, month two is when money appears.

Week 1 — build the funnel, write nothing public

Who What
Matt Buy the domain (name from docs/notes/brand-shortlist.md). Create the Instagram account on the imprint name. Create the Brevo account (free plan) and paste back the form URL — see The shelf is the ballot below.
Agent Write and build the email capture page. Write the first 10 Instagram cards. Design the card template — one font system, one colour system, one look, reused forever. Start writing book one.
Done when The capture page is live at the domain, and 10 posts are sitting ready.

Nothing is posted this week. Posting before the capture page exists throws away every visit.

Week 2 — start posting

Who What
Matt Post one card each weekday. Reply to every comment and DM. That is the whole job — about 15 minutes a day.
Agent Deliver the next week's 5 cards, each with 2-3 alternate hook lines. Keep writing book one.
Done when 5 posts live. First numbers exist: profile visits, bio-link clicks, signups.

Record three numbers every Friday: followers, bio-link clicks, email signups. These three replace the borrowed guesses in docs/distribution.md with real ones. This is the most valuable output of the whole month.

Week 3 — read the numbers, keep going

Who What
Matt Same: post daily, reply. Read Friday's three numbers.
Agent Next 5 cards. Book one draft continues. Rewrite the hooks based on which of week 2's posts actually travelled — this is the first real feedback we will ever have.
Done when 10 posts live, and we know which hook style works for us specifically.

Week 4 — finish the draft, set up the shop

Who What
Matt Same daily posting. Create the KDP account. Read book one as reader number one and say honestly whether it bored him.
Agent Next 5 cards. Finish the book one draft. Cut the free first-three-chapters version. Prepare the KDP listing — cover, blurb, and the narrow Kindle subcategories that are the only place an unknown imprint can rank.
Done when 20 posts live. Book one drafted and judged by Matt. KDP listing ready to upload.

Day 30 — the checkpoint

Look at three numbers and decide:

  • Followers. On track means climbing, not a specific figure. A faceless account from zero in 20 posts is small; that is expected.
  • Email signups. If people are following but not signing up, the funnel is broken — fix the bio link and the page, do not touch the content.
  • Matt's verdict on the book. If it bored him, it does not ship. That is the gate.

Then: upload to KDP, put the direct sale page up, and keep posting. The first stranger comes in month two.

The tripwire

From docs/distribution.md, unchanged and non-negotiable:

By day 60 — 40 posts — if the account is under 200 followers AND under 25 email signups, Instagram is wrong for us and we stop.

Day 30 is a checkpoint. Day 60 is a decision. Written down now so it is not argued with later.

Everything Matt must do himself

He holds every account and all the money. Nothing here can be delegated to an agent.

  1. Buy the domain.
  2. Create the Instagram account.
  3. Create the Brevo account and send back the form action URL (10 minutes, steps below).
  4. Create the KDP account.
  5. Post one card every weekday, and reply to comments. ~15 minutes a day. This is the one that decides whether any of it works.
  6. Read book one and say if it bored him.

The shelf is the ballot — how we learn what to write first

We have sixteen titles and no data behind their order. The shelf at /covers/ fixes that. Every cover is a checkbox: a reader taps the books they want, leaves one email, and the picks are stored with the address. Free, no payment, nothing to cancel — a waitlist, not a presale (DECISIONS.md).

What it buys us. Two numbers we cannot get any other way: which force people actually want explained, and how many books an interested person wants — one, or the shelf. The second number is what tells us whether the $69 bundle is real.

The rule for reading it. Ignore the counts until 50 people have voted. Under that, one Instagram post decides the winner. At 50, if the top title is not the current book one, we change the order and log it.

The one thing Matt must do — about 10 minutes:

  1. Sign up at brevo.com, free plan. No card.
  2. Contacts → create a list called Waitlist.
  3. Contacts → Settings → Contact attributes → add a text attribute named WANTS.
  4. Forms → create a form → choose the Simple HTML (no-JavaScript) embed. Add the WANTS field to it. Point it at the Waitlist list.
  5. Copy the action="https://sibforms.com/serve/..." URL out of the code it gives you and send it to the agent on Telegram.

Until that URL exists the shelf runs in demo mode: it validates the email, shows the reader a real confirmation, and stores nothing. Everything else on the page is finished.

What we do if it is not working

  • Nobody follows → the format or the topic is wrong on Instagram. Switch to the parked TikTok channel, on POV/fact hooks, which is what the research says wins there.
  • People follow, nobody signs up → funnel problem. One click, one page, one promise. Fix the page, not the posts.
  • People sign up, nobody buys → the book or the price is wrong. Ask the list directly; they are right there.
  • Matt cannot sustain the daily post → this is the real risk, and it is what killed every operator in the graveyard. The agent's job is to make posting take under a minute of decision: batch ready, hooks written, nothing to choose.

The thing this plan does not know

The topic. There is no search-volume evidence for The Dollar Empire — every keyword tool was blocked. It was chosen because it fits the promise, not because appetite was measured. The first two weeks of Instagram posts are, quietly, the topic test: if cards about the dollar do not travel, we learn it before the book is finished, not after.
