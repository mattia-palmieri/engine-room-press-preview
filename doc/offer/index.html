The three questions Matt asked, answered

Why Amazon at all?

Because Amazon supplies the stranger and we cannot. It is the one place a cold buyer finds us with no audience-building on our side, and it is the only place where reviews accumulate on a page a stranger already trusts. That is its whole job now: discovery and social proof, not margin.

The margin is better at home anyway. On a $9 ebook Amazon pays 70% minus a delivery fee of roughly $0.06 — about $6.24. Our own store on Payhip keeps about $8.55 at the same price. So Amazon is worth having for the traffic it brings, not for the money it pays, and every buyer we bring ourselves goes to our store.

Consequence, and it is the real decision here: no KDP Select. Select's 90-day exclusivity forbids selling the same ebook anywhere else, including our own site, beyond a 10% excerpt. That would kill the bundle, the free three chapters and the direct list in one move. We give up Kindle Unlimited page-reads (about $0.004–$0.005 a page) to keep the offer we actually push. Not close.

Paperback — in or out?

Out, for now. Print-on-demand costs nothing to set up, but every title needs its own interior PDF at trim size with bleed and its own cover spread with a spine width that depends on the final page count. That is real hours per book, sixteen times over, against Matt's 3–5 hours a week. And the payoff is not there: ebook and paperback carry separate Best Sellers Ranks, so a print edition does not lift the ebook at all. The credibility argument is real but unmeasured.

Revisit when one title is proven — a single paperback of the best-selling book, as a credibility object, not a format for the shelf.

How do you sell a bundle of sixteen books when one exists?

By never pretending otherwise. The bundle is sold as the shelf as it fills: pay $69 once, get every book that is out today by return, and every new one by email on the day it ships. The page states the true count next to the price — "4 of 16 published. The next is The Chokepoint, in three weeks." The discount ($69 against a $144 shelf) is what the reader is paid for waiting.

Two guards, because taking money for an unwritten book is where trust dies:

  1. An unconditional refund, any time until book sixteen ships. Not 14 days — the whole build period. It is a digital good with no marginal cost, and it removes the only real objection. It also keeps us clean of the EU withdrawal-right problem on prepaid digital goods.
  2. A public shipping record on the site — every book, its date, on time or late. If we slip, it says so.

Not doing: an Amazon preorder. KDP allows a one-year lead time, but missing the 72-hour final-file deadline costs preorder rights for a full year. We take no preorder on a book that is not written.

Not doing yet: the box-set ASIN on Amazon. It is allowed as a single product containing works we own, but publishing one before the component books exist is not confirmed as permitted anywhere official. Ship the box set on Amazon once the sixteen are real; until then the bundle lives at home.

The other decisions, unchanged

A series, shipped one at a time. The graveyard is unambiguous: a one-shot book has no backlist to compound. catalogue.md names all sixteen from day one, so the imprint reads as a shelf immediately. We still write exactly one at a time. Book one is Money, per the catalogue's publishing order.

Short: ~20,000 words, an evening's read. The complaint we aim at is too dense, could not absorb it. Length is the complaint. It also lets book two exist this year, which the graveyard says matters more than book one being perfect.

The free thing is three real chapters, not a bonus PDF. The best predictor of someone buying our book is having enjoyed reading our book. Give them a third of it, ending on the question the rest answers.

What is deliberately NOT in the ladder

A paid curriculum site. The most interesting gap we have — and the heaviest possible build. The coming-soon catalogue with per-title interest capture tests the appetite without building it (presale in the queue).

A paid newsletter. Slowest path to a first sale, and it needs a personality we have ruled out.

A subscription "new book each month." A cadence we would break, and a broken cadence is exactly how the graveyard operators died quietly.

Patreon. Needs an ongoing show. We are not making a show.

What has to be true

  1. Book one is genuinely good. Matt is reader one; if it bores him it ships to nobody.
  2. The free three chapters are the real opening, not a re-cut.
  3. The email capture page exists before the first Instagram post.
  4. The KDP listing wins a narrow Kindle subcategory. A subcategory #1 with 38 ratings is reachable; the World History top 20 is not.
  5. We actually ship a book every three weeks. The bundle promise is a delivery promise, and the refund guarantee makes breaking it expensive.

The honest risk

The bundle asks a stranger to trust a shelf that does not exist yet, from an imprint with no track record. The refund removes the money risk but not the credibility one, so the first four books have to ship on time — the shipping record on the site is either our best proof or our public failure.

And the topic order is still judgement: every search-volume tool was blocked, so we have no demand data behind Money going first. The waitlist (presale) is the test.
