Two decisions. Pick one of each.

You said the shelf page is ugly on a desktop — too much empty space, no hierarchy — and that the covers themselves feel undesigned. Those are two separate fixes, so here are two separate choices. D is the new one — copied from real book shops, measured on a phone, as you asked. Everything below is real: all sixteen books, real blurbs, working pages.

Reply with a letter and a number, like “B and 02”.

Decision 1The desktop layout

Option A

The shelf

The covers get big and sit four across, like a bookshop table. Each one carries only its title and its one line; the blurb hides behind a click. The page sells the covers.

Cost: you have to click to read a blurb.

Option B

The ledger

One line per book, straight across the full width: number, cover, title, blurb. Nothing is hidden and nothing is ragged. It reads like a catalogue, which is what it is.

Cost: the covers stay small.

Option C

The spread

One book per band, the cover big on the left and the whole entry set beside it. Each book gets a moment. The cover pins itself while you read.

Cost: the longest page — sixteen bands of scrolling.

Option D

The bookshop

You said not to reinvent the wheel. So this one is not invented — it is measured. I opened Fitzcarraldo, Standard Ebooks, Persephone, Verso and New York Review Books in a real browser at phone width and read the numbers off the page. All five do the same thing: two covers across on a phone, four on a desktop, a tight gap, the title and nothing else under the cover, and the blurb never in the grid — always one tap away. This copies that, on our covers.

Cost: the blurb is a tap away, and it is the plainest of the four.
