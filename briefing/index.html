The reader

Not "curious adults" — too big to aim at. The person who bounced off history at school and now can't follow the news, because nobody explained the machinery. 25–45, reads non-fiction to understand the world. They don't want to become a history person; they want to stop feeling ignorant.

Evidence: 17 Reddit threads asking for "a book like Sapiens". The complaint is always the same — too dense, too fast, "bored out of my mind". They want history they can finish.

Not for: hardcore history fans, and students.

The three promises

  • One idea per book.
  • No dates to memorise.
  • You will finish it.

Distribution — Instagram, alone

Five static image cards a week, one bio link to one email page. TikTok and YouTube parked, not dropped.

Why: Instagram is the only one of the three where anyone in this niche visibly collects emails (@geopolitical.guy, 377K, bio → Substack, one click). Not one TikTok winner had any route off-platform. Static cards are also the cheapest thing an agent can make, and faceless accounts here run 74.8K → 6.3M followers.

The funnel

reach → profile visit → bio click (3%) → signup (30%) → sale (5%)

~450 profile visits = one paying stranger. At five posts a week from zero, expect the first sale in month two or three, not week one.

Kill tripwire

Day 60, 40 posts in: under 200 followers AND under 25 signups → Instagram is wrong, we stop. Every dead operator in the graveyard stopped quietly with no decision point written down. This is the decision point.

Amazon KDP first, because Amazon is the only model that hands you a stranger for free. Direct sale alongside (nets $4.40). Books two and three named before book one ships — the World Wars, the British Empire — so it reads as a series from day one.

Ruled out: paid newsletter (slowest to first sale, needs a personality), monthly subscription (a cadence we'd break), Patreon, and the curriculum site (real gap, but the heaviest build — it arrives when there are 3–4 books to sequence).

Is it viable? The honest read

  • Good: the demand is evidenced and specific, the reader's own complaint words are free to use, faceless works at scale on Instagram, and KDP means a stranger can buy without us building an audience first.
  • Crowded: "one topic explains the world" is a busy format. Our edge has to be the topic and the hook, not the format.
  • Unproven #1: no demand evidence for The Dollar Empire specifically — every keyword tool was blocked. It was picked because it fits, not because it was measured. The first two weeks of posts are the real topic test.
  • Unproven #2: every funnel rate above is borrowed from outside the niche. It's a plan for what to measure, not a forecast.
  • The real constraint is Matt. 15 minutes a day, five days a week, for 60 days. The graveyard says burnout is what kills these, not strategy.
